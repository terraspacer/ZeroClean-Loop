# V2: weak sources fit, but strict orientation sensitivity does not support repairs

The separate v2 model allows a source to be below one half in exact-string match probability and even below multiclass chance. All 920 preregistered conditional fits converged; none failed a numerical-boundary gate. The lower-bound pathology seen in v1's actual-time attributes therefore disappears under this parameterization. No proposed repair survives the full unchanged candidate, orientation, leave-group-out, and source-assumption requirements. No output was applied to the live model.

This is an assumption-bound dirty-only experiment. It uses no correctness labels, benchmark references, error masks, evaluation outcomes, prior external predictions, or answer catalogs. Frozen v1 artifacts were preserved and their hashes rechecked after v2. The 116 v1 common proposals are still available, but they do not pass the broader preregistered sensitivity.

## Mathematical change and validation

For K observed candidates plus one unobserved state, v2 uses `q = exp(theta) / (exp(theta) + K)`. Positive theta exceeds chance `1/(K+1)`; negative theta is permitted. At theta `log(2)` and K=3, q=0.4 despite exceeding chance 0.25. A Gaussian penalty supplies a disclosed soft orientation preference, with means `-log(2)`, zero, and `log(2)` tested separately and standard deviation 2. All starts -2, zero, and 2 are retained. An additional two unseen-state-prior fits and twelve bootstrap fits give 23 sensitivity fits per fold. The [preregistered protocol](latent_consensus_v2_protocol.md) gives the likelihood, M-step derivative, fixed gates, and limitations.

Four new synthetic checks passed before any v2 dirty fit: multiclass chance arithmetic; a penalized M-step derivative permitting negative skill; monotone EM on mixed good, weak, adverse, and copied three-category sources; and abstention when changing orientation or the unobserved state changes the winner. The complete focused suite passes 14 tests. Synthetic labels were generated by construction and did not tune real-table thresholds.

V2 retains v1's exact dirty strings, duplicate handling, conflicting-source exclusion, entity folds, source clusters, support gates, 0.98 candidate weight, 0.20 margin, and 0.95 leave-group-out weight. It changes the likelihood and source parameter penalty; it does not loosen those acceptance thresholds.

The ten supported clustered scheduled-departure entities yield 178 rows whose original string agrees with the candidate, with no proposed changes. At a fixed positive or neutral Gaussian mean, three-start agreement holds for 21 of 30 eligible clustered scheduled-departure entities; at the negative mean it holds for 13. For the other attribute/source combinations, no eligible entity has the same winner across all three starts even at one fixed prior mean. Changing the prior is therefore not the sole source of instability.

Source skill signs also change across fits. For example, actual-departure independent-name fits contain 9 to 25 negative-skill sources, and clustered fits contain 4 to 8. Negative skill is below categorical chance under this model; it does not prove those sources are empirically wrong or malicious. In independent-name scheduled departure, 53 of 100 eligible entities have the unobserved category win in at least one sensitivity fit. This further illustrates the dependence on orientation and vocabulary assumptions.

## Convergence is not identification

All fits satisfy the fixed skill-change convergence rule. The smallest checked EM objective increment over the 920 fits is about -1.8e-12, consistent with floating-point noise. This verifies the implementation's penalized objective update, not a global optimum.

The protocol deliberately retains every converged start, including much worse stationary solutions. Under the same prior mean, independent-name objective spreads across starts range from roughly 394 to 668 log-objective units. Clustered-source spreads range from numerical equality to about 172. Consequently, strict abstention here is an optimizer and orientation stress-test result. It does **not** establish that the alternative truths have equal likelihood, that a source sign is fundamentally unidentifiable, or that every potential repair lacks support under a better optimized model.

This distinction matters: retaining every start is conservative but can make the finite sensitivity envelope much wider than likelihood-supported uncertainty. It is not a posterior over stationary fits and supplies no calibrated error guarantee. The experiment does demonstrate that choosing a preferred initialization or soft prior without exposing the other modes would hide substantial sensitivity.

## Inspectable artifacts and next steps

- [Summary](latent_consensus_flights_preview_v2/summary.json): all per-attribute/source-assumption status counts and fold diagnostics.
- [Sensitivity diagnostics](latent_consensus_flights_preview_v2/sensitivity_diagnostics.json): entity agreement, negative source skills, sign changes, row abstention reasons, and unobserved-state winners.
- [Local-optimum diagnostics](latent_consensus_flights_preview_v2/local_optimum_diagnostics.json): per-fold, per-prior objective spreads and preferred start by objective.
- [Artifact hashes](latent_consensus_flights_preview_v2/artifact_manifest.json): provenance checks; compressed files retain all row decisions, exact original strings, source skills, and full objective traces.

The supported next experiment is to preregister an optimization rule that selects the best penalized objective and defines a near-optimal set before fitting, then repeat orientation and source-group sensitivity within that set. An objective-gap cutoff would be a modeling choice requiring justification; selecting it to recover desired coverage would invalidate the experiment. Keep the same candidate/support gates and preserve these v1/v2 results. A model with an external, dirty-derived vocabulary fixed independently of held-out claims would also permit a more coherent held-source predictive check.

Observation dates, source lineage, time-zone and field semantics, nonignorable missingness, and legitimate alternate formats remain unresolved. Shared systematic errors remain possible under either source assumption. The five tables without source provenance remain outside this source-reliability experiment. Neither v1 nor v2 establishes a general unsupervised ED/EC improvement across the six benchmarks.
